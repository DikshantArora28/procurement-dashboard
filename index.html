Impact Assessment Methodology

How disruption severity levels are determined

Each plant disruption is assessed using a multi-factor scoring model that evaluates the potential market impact. The severity level (High, Medium, Low) reflects the combined effect of capacity loss, event type, duration, and market conditions.

Scoring Factors

40% Capacity as % of Global Supply

The affected plant's nameplate capacity as a percentage of total global capacity for that product. Higher share = greater market impact.

>2% = +3 pts 0.5-2% = +2 pts <0.5% = +1 pt
25% Event Type (Planned vs Unplanned)

Unplanned outages have higher impact because markets have not priced in the supply loss, causing sudden tightness and price volatility.

Unplanned = +3 pts Force Majeure = +2 pts Planned = +1 pt
20% Estimated Duration

Longer outages compound supply loss. Extended shutdowns can deplete regional inventories and trigger contract force majeure clauses.

>30 days = +3 pts 7-30 days = +2 pts <7 days = +1 pt
15% Downstream Chain Effect

Upstream disruptions (e.g., crackers) cascade into multiple derivative products. A cracker outage affects PE, PP, EO, EG, styrene and more.

Cracker/Refinery = +3 pts Intermediate = +2 pts End product = +1 pt

Impact Thresholds

LevelScore RangeVisualCriteria Summary
HIGH 8-12 pts Red + Pulse Large capacity (>1M t/y), unplanned, cracker/refinery, extended duration
MEDIUM 5-7 pts Amber Mid-size capacity (300K-1M t/y), planned turnaround in key region
LOW 1-4 pts Green Small capacity (<300K t/y), planned, short duration, surplus market
